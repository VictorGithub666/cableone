5 Signs It's Time to Upgrade Your Home WiFi

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Upgrade Your WiFi
July 5, 2026 5 min read WiFi

There's a particular kind of frustration that comes from watching a loading circle spin in the middle of a movie night, or hearing "you're frozen, can you hear me?" on a work call for the third time this week. If that sounds familiar, the problem usually isn't your laptop, your phone, or your smart TV, it's the WiFi plan trying to keep up with a home that has quietly outgrown it.

Most households don't upgrade their internet until something breaks the routine, a missed meeting, a ruined series finale, a teenager complaining that their game keeps lagging. Here are five reliable signs that your home has reached that point, and what a better-matched plan actually looks like.

1. Video calls freeze at the worst possible moment

If your camera pixelates or your audio cuts out the second someone else in the house starts streaming, downloading, or gaming, your connection is being asked to do more than it was built for. Entry-level plans are designed for light browsing and social media on one or two devices, not simultaneous video calls, cloud backups, and 4K playback.

2. Everyone in the house is fighting for bandwidth

Count the devices connected to your router right now, phones, laptops, smart TVs, a games console, maybe a smart speaker or two. Every one of them draws from the same pool of speed. A household with three or four people online at once needs meaningfully more capacity than one with a single occupant checking email.

A quick way to tell

  • Videos take longer than a few seconds to start playing
  • Pages load noticeably slower in the evening, when everyone is home
  • Downloads that should take minutes are taking the better part of an hour
Every CableOne WiFi package runs on the same fibre network with unlimited data, the difference between tiers is simply how much speed your household needs at once.

3. Streaming looks worse than it should

If Netflix, YouTube, or your favourite streaming app keeps dropping from HD to a blurry standard-definition picture, that's usually your connection automatically stepping down to avoid buffering. It's a smart failsafe, but it also means the resolution you're paying your streaming subscription for isn't the resolution you're actually watching.

4. Your smart home devices lag behind

Smart CCTV, smart plugs, video doorbells and voice assistants all sit quietly on your network in the background, checking in constantly. Add a couple of these to a household that's already stretched thin on bandwidth, and it's often the reason a previously "fine" connection suddenly starts to feel sluggish.

5. You've upgraded everything except the internet

New phone, new smart TV, new laptop, but the WiFi package is the same one you signed up for years ago. Devices get faster every year and are built to take advantage of higher speeds. If your plan hasn't changed while everything connected to it has, it's worth checking whether you're leaving performance on the table.
